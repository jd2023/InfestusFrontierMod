# S05 — Passive logistics networks

## Contract

Veins establish connectivity. Storage owns resources. Loaded producers and
consumers transfer directly through compatible endpoints. Known passive connections
remain usable while their chunks are unloaded; unloaded inventories and machines
remain unavailable. Unknown unloaded terrain creates no connection. Loading a known
chunk invalidates its cached component before its blocks are used again.

No chunk tickets, per-vein inventories, per-vein ticks, packet travel simulation or
recursive capability forwarding. No distance penalty in this version. Existing
filters, reserves, port directions, crossings and stitches retain their meaning.
Gas processing and long-distance relay organs remain separate future content.

## Steps

1. **Shared graph.** Add incremental, bounded component discovery in core, with
   shared results, cycle handling, targeted invalidation and explicit overflow.
   Test limits, contention, rebuilds, independent components and bounded work.
2. **World topology.** Persist observed connections through vanilla SavedData.
   Discover only loaded blocks or saved passive connections. Invalidate on changes
   and chunk load; do not erase knowledge on unload. Bound saved observations and
   outstanding discovery requests. Add lifecycle and no-chunk-loading GameTests.
3. **Items.** Replace per-Mouth route searches and timed packets with shared network
   membership and immediate endpoint transactions. Retain only bounded undelivered
   remainder at the sending Mouth. Preserve filters, priorities, reserves and sided
   inventories. Replace travel-time tests with transaction/conservation tests.
4. **Liquids.** Remove vein block entities and recursive crossing/stitch handlers.
   Expose a live network capability for fill/drain against loaded endpoints. Keep
   Junctions as active transfer organs and finite buffers. Apply shared admission,
   reentrancy protection, media checks and bounded endpoint offers. Replace pipe
   packet tests with passive-network tests.
5. **Integration.** Update the guide, diagnostics, engineering contracts and design
   requirements. Exercise the farm/furnace loop, loops, blocked destinations,
   source/destination unload, unloaded middle connections, reload and graph limits.
   Search production and tests for all superseded transport concepts and remove them.
6. **Qualification.** Run formatting, core tests, GameTests and the full gate. Commit
   logical steps separately. Fresh review follows REVIEW.md; repair findings and
   push only after approval.

## Bounds

- One local component: 1,024 logical cells, with crossings counted per passage.
- Discovery queue: 256 origins per dimension. No duplicate queued origin.
- Component cache: at most 65,536 indexed cells per dimension; evict complete caches
  when full, without deleting stored resources or observed connections.
- Observed topology: at most 65,536 positions per dimension, persisted without any
  inventory data. Refuse discovery beyond this bound; removing known cells frees it.
- Discovery: 2,048 node expansions per dimension per tick, 8,192 per server tick.
- Endpoint transactions: 64 admitted operations per dimension per tick, 256 per
  server tick. Each operation has at most 16 destination offers and bounded slot
  inspection. Simulation consumes work admission as well as execution.
- A Mouth retains at most one remainder of 8 items. Junction capacity remains in its
  existing contract. Passive routes retain nothing.

## Verification

- Core: rings, exact and exceeded limits, incremental work, invalidation during a
  search, shared cache lookup, independent networks and full request queues.
- Game: direct item/liquid transfers, simulated operations leave balances unchanged,
  loops and crossings, reserves, competing outputs, full destinations, retained
  remainders, changing topology, loaded endpoint guards and normal restart state.
- Chunk tests: cached middle unloaded continues; unknown middle unloaded refuses;
  endpoint unloaded refuses; loading changed topology invalidates before transfer.
- Performance: record bounded operation counts and measured dense-network runtime;
  report scope rather than claiming multiplayer or crash qualification from unit tests.
